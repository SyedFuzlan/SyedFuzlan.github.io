My story

The story
so far

I saw the finished thing years before I could build one. This is the road from there to here, including the parts that stalled.

  1. '21'21

    Where it started

    B.Tech CSE begins. I spent four years at the surface: able to follow any conversation, able to finish almost nothing.

    FCSE

    @btech5 years ago

    '21

    Where it started

    The degree gave me range and no depth. I could talk about systems, AI, security, and then freeze the moment someone asked me to build one. Naming that plainly is the only reason the years after it look different.

  2. '22'22

    Tech Code Clan, from zero

    Founded a student tech community and took it from 0 to 150+ members. Mentored 50+ students through their first real builds.

    FTC

    @techcodeclan4 years ago

    '22

    Tech Code Clan, from zero

    No budget, no faculty mandate, no template. I recruited the first twenty people by walking up to them. What stuck: people do not join a plan, they join something already moving. Sessions ran weekly whether four showed up or forty.

  3. '24'24

    CySEntia, 1,000+ in the room

    Ran the CySEntia tech festival end to end. 1,000+ participants, one student team, one semester.

    FCS

    @cysentia2 years ago

    '24

    CySEntia, 1,000+ in the room

    Sponsors, speakers, scheduling, registrations, and the part nobody sees: making the day boring for everyone except the people attending. It was the closest thing to shipping a product I had done: a hard date, a real audience, and no way to quietly extend the deadline.

  4. Lead credential

    '25'25

    2nd place at IIT Madras

    2nd place, IIT Madras AI Startup Competition. Top 60 of 6,000+ entries. Pitched health-tech to a panel that included a former Google VP.

    FIIT

    @iitmadras1 year ago

    '25

    2nd place at IIT Madras

    Six thousand entries in, sixty out, then a stage and a short window to make the case. The questions were about the business, not the model. Second place, and a place on a 12-day entrepreneurship program after it. I lead with this one because it was judged by people who had nothing to gain from being kind.

  5. '25'25

    M.Tech, and the first paid work

    M.Tech CSE begins. Freelance AI work starts alongside it: training data and model evaluation, on deadlines that were not mine.

    FMT

    @mtech1 year ago

    '25

    M.Tech, and the first paid work

    Labelling and evaluation work is not glamorous and it taught me more about how models actually fail than any course did. It also paid for the machine this site was built on. Working to someone else’s spec, on someone else’s clock, is where the "ships" line in the trait list came from.

  6. Nov '25Nov '25

    Into SmashVision as a data labeller

    Joined SmashVision, a Belgian sports-AI startup, annotating volleyball footage frame by frame. Then asked for more scope, three times.

    FSV

    @smashvision10 months ago

    Nov '25

    Into SmashVision as a data labeller

    Frame-by-frame annotation is unglamorous and exact. You notice the model’s blind spots before anyone tells you. I asked to help outside the brief three separate times before the actual video work started landing. Scope you are given is a starting point, not a ceiling.

  7. '26'26

    Video Studio, and a store that went live

    Built an AI studio that edits by conversation and delivered 10 films to SmashVision through it. Shipped madebyzohra.in, rebuilding its backend from Medusa to plain Express.

    FZLAN

    @zlanthis year

    '26

    Video Studio, and a store that went live

    ZLAN edits by conversation instead of by timeline: transcript in, edit decision list out, base cut, motion composition, render. Ten finished films went to SmashVision through it. The store was two weeks of the least glamorous work I have done. I deleted a whole Medusa backend and wrote a plain Express one because the complexity was not earning its keep.

  8. Jul '26Jul '26

    Running marketing with a team of agents

    Volunteered to run SmashVision’s marketing with no marketing background, so I built the team out of AI agents, with a gate that refuses any claim without a source.

    FMKT

    @smashvision2 months ago

    Jul '26

    Running marketing with a team of agents

    I had never run marketing before and said yes anyway. The honest fix was not to fake expertise. It was to build agents that draft, and one that blocks anything unverifiable before it goes out. The gate has rejected more of my own claims than anyone else’s.

  9. Aug '26Aug '26

    A second client, and systems that check each other

    Cut nine reels from one Essae Chandran Institute shoot, one commissioned and paid. Built Pair, where Claude Code and Codex review each other, and Mirror, one memory for all my agents.

    FECI

    @ecilast month

    Aug '26

    A second client, and systems that check each other

    One shoot, nine reels, because a single day of footage is worth more cut nine different ways than once. Pair and Mirror came out of the same instinct as the marketing gate: agents left unchecked drift, so I built the checking in: one tool reviews another’s work, and one memory keeps every agent working from the same facts.

  10. NOWNOW

    The honest card

    The pattern I keep hitting: architecture gets fully designed, execution stalls. I have named it, and I build against it now.

    FF

    @fuzlantoday

    NOW

    The honest card

    Syan is the clearest case: fully specified, and not a line of production code. It happened twice before that. What changed is the method, not the ambition: pick the smallest thing that can actually be finished, put a hard date on it, and show it whether or not I would call it good enough. Published beats perfect.
